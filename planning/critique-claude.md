# Critique (Claude) of the Codex and Copilot plans

Written after reading `plan-codex.md` in full and `plan-copilot.md` §§0–2 (its §§3–5 were still being
written). Both plans are stronger than mine in places; I say where.

## Codex plan — "Words at Work"

**Borrow**
1. *A correct answer must change a shared situation.* Every round ends in a consequence in the world
   (stones arrive, a window closes), not a red ✗. This is the sharpest statement of the quiz risk in any of
   the three plans, and it became the test I apply to every round.
2. *Repair Café.* Same words, changed situation, different right response — PI §43 with its caveat
   ("for a large class of cases — though not for all") played, not lectured. Paired rounds
   ("It's a bit cold in here" with the window open / with the cup empty) are the best single teaching device
   in the three plans. Adopted as game 3.
3. *Scene Machine scoring by relations, not coordinates*, and its honest limit ("a deliberately limited
   spatial analogy, not a complete account of the Tractatus"). Adopted: the stage's truth is computed from a
   logical model; art never decides a mark. Also: separate generation of backgrounds and sprites.

**Flaws**
1. *No audio at all.* The builders' game is a game of calls; dropping listening removes the most natural
   English skill it trains. Speech synthesis costs nothing and the text is always on screen, so the
   accessibility worry is met. Kept TTS as optional.
2. *A paper-prototype gate with five learners before any build* is right for a funded team and impossible in
   this session; treated as a recommendation for the owner, not a precondition.
3. *54 rounds with two success routes each* over-commits authoring. The real constraint is review time per
   round; 12–18 strong rounds per game beat 54 thin ones. Café rounds use one fitting response plus an
   always-free "Ask first" route instead of two full routes.

## Copilot plan

**Borrow**
1. *Go On! (teach the pupil)* — the best new mechanic in any plan. You hold the rule; a pupil with several
   "ways of going on" picks one consistent with your examples; an Explain card is itself misread (§198); the
   "1000" moment asks the teacher's call. It trains real learner errors (a/an by sound, plural spelling,
   -ed doubling) and turns PI §§185–202 into play without sceptical overreach. Adopted as game 4.
2. *English notes that name the rule in one sentence* ("After *two*, English needs the plural"). My Slab!
   auto-pluralised tiles, so it never practised the plural it claims to teach. Fix: offer singular and plural
   tiles and coach the slip without blocking the build.
3. *Family Likeness with quantifiers* (all / most / not all; scope of negation) — the strongest English target
   for §§66–67 and directly useful for exam writing. My version only chained cards; Copilot's "Claim" round
   with an Objector holding the counterexample is better. Candidate game 5.

**Flaws**
1. *Three games, Logical Space gated* — drops the Tractatus (and the early/late contrast) from v1, which the
   brief's "Tractatus" citation implies. Picture Theatre keeps TLP 2.1/2.21/4.01 in v1 at low cost.
2. *Par and "no more orders than par"* turns a cooperative game into optimisation pressure; fine for strong
   players, but it should not cost a mark for B1 learners. Marks here: done + first try, no par.
3. *Plan breadth* (`/notes` articles, `/privacy`, `/terms`, 404 fly-bottle, eight concepts) — good ideas, but
   each page is a surface to keep accurate. The fly-bottle 404 is cheap and on-theme; the rest wait.

## Where my plan was weakest
- My Slab! had a countdown timer — Codex and Copilot both reject timers; they are right (anxiety, not
  learning). Removed.
- My "Must, Might, Can't" hinge game is the highest interpretive risk (Codex says the same of its Doubt Desk);
  it moves to the backlog.
- My plan had no story for "meaning is use" beyond Slab!; the Café fills that gap.

## Recommended v1
Slab! · Picture Theatre · The Café · Go On! — four distinct verbs (fetch/command, arrange/describe,
respond, teach) and four English targets (orders & number, prepositions, pragmatics, morphology). Family
Likeness if time allows.
